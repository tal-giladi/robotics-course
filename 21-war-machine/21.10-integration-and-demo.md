## What you will learn

- Build the **test matrix**: every (trigger × releaser × payload) combination, with a minimum shot count and a pass criterion — and why the demo uses a subset that the matrix proves.
- Lay out the **test range**: measured distances, the target zones, the cannon's ceiling constraint, the baselines (lighting, noise, wind), and the spectator line.
- Run the **12-step demo runbook** with a go/no-go at each step — a demo is a test with an audience, and the audience is the least safe component.
- Log every shot as a **CSV row** and analyse the demo: hit rate, false fires, jams, pack voltage delta — the numbers that replace the (estimate) rows in the spec delta.
- Package the **evidence**: the CSV, the state-machine traces, the range photos, and the measured spec delta — the module's final artifact, with the course files untouched.

## Why it matters

Integration is the part where the robot stops being a collection of lessons and becomes one
machine with a job. Every subsystem was proven alone (21.03's 20 releases, 21.04's range
day, 21.05's 50 m RC test, 21.06's 60 s idle, 21.08's sag campaign, 21.09's interlock
matrix) — and the integration is where the *combinations* live: the trigger that the
releaser has never seen, the payload that the matrix has not logged, the pack that is at
60% when the demo starts. The test matrix is the proof that the combinations work; the
range is the stage that makes the proof honest (measured distances, baselines, the
spectator line); and the runbook is the 12 steps that keep the demo a *test* instead of a
performance — because a demo is a test with an audience, and the audience leans in.

## Concept

Three artifacts, in order: the **matrix** (what must be true), the **range** (where the
truth is measured), the **runbook** (how the truth is performed). The matrix is exhaustive
(every combination, or a smoke of it); the range is physical (the distances, the baselines,
the line the audience stands behind); the runbook is the 12 steps with a go/no-go at each
— and the go/no-go is *evidence*, not a feeling (step 1 is the pack V against your measured
R_total, step 3 is the LiDAR scan, step 4 is the slot sensors saying LOADED).

The demo's audience is the least safe component: they lean in at step 8 (the launch class),
they clap at step 10 (the sound shot — early, if the cue is not a spoken word), and they
are in the smoke at step 10's aftermath (the glasses are the mitigation, 21.09 Level 4).
The runbook exists so that the *robot* is the safe component, and the audience's job is to
watch.

> [!TIP]
> **Ask your teacher:** "Why does the demo use a subset of the matrix, when the matrix is
> the proof?" (Answer in Level 1: the matrix proves the *combinations* at 3 shots each —
> the smoke test — and the demo's 10 shots are the *show*, not the proof. A combination
> that passes 3/3 in the matrix is *allowed* to be in the demo; the demo is where the
> allowed combinations are performed in front of the audience. The matrix is the
> admission ticket, the demo is the performance.)

## Technical explanation

### Level 1 — the test matrix

The rows are (trigger, releaser, payload) combinations; the columns are the evidence:

| Column | What it is | Pass |
|---|---|---|
| shots_fired | the count (10 for the demo set, 3 for the smoke) | = the plan |
| shots_landed | in the target zone (Level 2's geometry) | ≥ 8/10 (demo set) |
| false_fires | a fire with no trigger event (the gate, the crowd, the sun) | 0 |
| jams | the 2 s timeout (21.01 rule 3) | 0 unresolved |
| pack_V_before / after | the INA219, per shot (21.08-E3's method) | ΔV < 0.5 V for the show |

The minimums: **10 shots** per combination you will use in the demo; **3 shots** per
combination you will not (the smoke test — enough to catch a wiring error, not enough to
estimate a hit rate). For the **Scout** package: 2 triggers (keypad, RC) × 3 releasers
(drop bin, pin, slingshot) × 4 payloads (stink bomb stand-in, balloon, magnet, bouncy ball
drop) = **24 combinations × 3 smoke shots = 72 shots** — a full afternoon, which is why
the demo uses a subset (10 shots, 2-3 combinations) and the matrix proves the subset. The
Assault adds the cannon (× its 2-3 payloads, × the RC trigger) — the matrix grows, and the
afternoon becomes two.

The CSV is the matrix's memory: one row per shot, `t, trigger, weapon, payload,
pack_V_before, state_trace, hit/miss, notes` — the state_trace is the Weapon FSM's log
(21.01's `Weapon.log`: the (t, state) pairs), and the hit/miss is the *target zone* of
Level 2, not "it went somewhere". The CSV is the evidence package's core (Level 4).

### Level 3 — the demo runbook (12 steps, go/no-go at each)

The runbook is the 20.06 culture (the course's own demo discipline) with the weapon rows
of 21.09. Every step has a GO/NO-GO and the evidence; a NO-GO stops the demo at that step
(the audience hears "we are checking X" — the honesty is part of the show).

| Step | Action | GO/NO-GO (the evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Power check | pack V against your measured R_total (21.08-E3): GO if V ≥ 11.0 V (the 56% solenoid-force case is at 8.95 V — the demo starts with margin); the INA219 is logging |
| 2 | E-stop test, in front of the audience | press, watch the bus die (the multimeter, or the LED on the 12 V tap), release, confirm no motion (21.09-E3 test 1/8); the witness nods |
| 3 | Rail check | no loose M3 (the finger test), the LiDAR scan shows no new furniture (the 21.09 phantom-wall row), the magnet sweep is clean (21.09 row 13) |
| 4 | Payload load + verify | every slot's sensor says LOADED (21.02 Level 4 — LOADED is a verified fact); the 30 min rule (the balloon is loaded 30 min before the demo, not 3 h — the leak row, 21.09 #12) |
| 5 | Arm | the FSM says ARMED, the e-stop is out (the 21.09 test 8: the e-stop out is not an arm — the arm is explicit) |
| 6 | Fire 1 — drop class, the safe one | the keypad (the operator's hands, the audience's first "oh"); the payload lands in the 0.5 m zone |
| 7 | Confirm + log | the release sensor confirms (SAFE), the CSV row is written, the state_trace is logged |
| 8 | Fire 2 — launch class, the audience leans in | the slingshot (the Scout) or the cannon (the Assault) at the 3 m board; the 15/25 m clearing is respected (the audience is behind the line) |
| 9 | Vision-triggered fire — the "magic" shot | the ArUco marker at 2.5 m (21.06's E3), the gate completes (0.5 s), the payload drops in the zone; the audience sees the robot *decide* |
| 10 | Sound-triggered fire — the crowd claps | the cue is a *spoken word* ("clap code now"), the crowd claps, the double-clap fires (21.07); the e-stop is in the operator's hand (the crowd claps early — the runbook's cue is the guard) |
| 11 | Reload check | empty slots (the sensors say EMPTY/SPENT), the spent cartridge count (the CO2: the 12 g count, vented outside — 21.09 Level 3) |
| 12 | Stand-down | disarm (the FSM SAFE), the e-stop in (the bus dead), the pack out (the 20.02 rule: the Li-ion is a fuel tank — it does not sit on the range overnight) |

The step order is the safety order: the safe class first (step 6 — the drop, the payload
that falls in front of the robot), the launch class second (step 8 — the audience has seen
the safe one and knows the pattern), the magic shots last (steps 9-10 — the vision and the
sound, the ones that can surprise). The smoke, if it is in the demo, is the *last* payload
of step 10's aftermath (21.09 row 22 — the shared medium, the audience's glasses).

### Level 4 — the data and the evidence

Every shot is a CSV row (Level 1's schema). After the demo, the analysis:

- **Hit rate per combination** (shots_landed / shots_fired, per row of the matrix) — the
  demo set's ≥ 8/10 is the pass; a combination at 6/10 is a finding (the 21.04 drag
  factor, the 21.03 flap angle, the 21.06 gate latency — the CSV's notes column is where
  the finding goes).
- **False-fire count** (target 0) — a false fire is a fire with no trigger event (the
  gate's shadow, the crowd's early clap, the sun on the IR beacon) — the 21.06/21.07
  idle tests are the *pre*-demo proof, the demo's false-fire count is the *in*-show
  measurement.
- **Jam count** (target 0 unresolved) — a jam is the 2 s timeout (21.01 rule 3); an
  *unresolved* jam is a payload whose location is unknown at stand-down (step 12's pack
  out does not happen until the payload is found — the 21.09 big-three's jam is the
  physical mystery).
- **Pack voltage delta** (pack_V_after − pack_V_before, the whole show) — expect < 0.5 V
  (the firing is ~1% of the pack per 100 shots — 21.01; a 30-minute show with 10 shots
  fires 0.3% of the pack, and the drive's 17 W × 0.5 h is the 8.5 Wh that moves the
  voltage — the delta is the *drive's*, not the firing's, and it is the 21.08 runtime
  model in one number).

The **evidence package** (the module's final artifact, and the course files stay
untouched — the spec delta is in *this* module, 21.01 Level 5): the CSV (every shot),
the state-machine traces (every shot's FSM log), the range photos (the layout, the target
zones, the spectator line), and the **measured spec delta** (the 21.01 Level 5 table with
every (estimate) replaced by the 21.08-E3 / 21.09-E3 / 21.04-E3 measurements — the mass
row is the scale, the sag row is the INA219, the range row is the tape). The evidence
package is what "the war machine is done" means: not "it fired", but "it fired, and the
numbers are here".

The apex computation (Level 2's ceiling constraint), inline:

```python
import math
v = 15.5  # m/s, the cannon's muzzle velocity (21.04)
for angle in (45, 30, 29):
    apex = v ** 2 * math.sin(math.radians(angle)) ** 2 / (2 * 9.81)
    rng = v ** 2 * math.sin(math.radians(2 * angle)) / 9.81
    print(f"{angle:2d} deg: apex {apex:4.1f} m  range {rng:5.1f} m")
```

```text
45 deg: apex  6.1 m  range  24.5 m
30 deg: apex  3.4 m  range  21.2 m
29 deg: apex  3.1 m  range  20.8 m
```

The 3 m ceiling allows 29° (apex 3.1 m is over the ceiling — the net or the outdoors day;
the 21.04 Q4's "≤ 29°" is the *ceiling* constraint, and the range at 29° is still 20.8 m,
more than the 3-5 m boards need).

## Troubleshooting

### Symptom: the matrix is all red (every combination 1/3)

The parts are not the problem — the *integration* is: power (the shared GND — the 20.02
domain split was not made, the compute GND and the weapon GND are not the same star), pin
allocation (two peripherals on one GPIO — the 21.05/21.07's GPIO 16 conflict, the RC RX
and the I²S BCLK), the e-stop wiring (the 21.09 test 3's NC-on-the-wrong-side). The
all-red matrix is an integration failure, and the 21.09-E3 matrix (the 10 interlock tests)
is the diagnostic: run it before re-running the 72 shots.

### Symptom: the vision shot fails on the range but worked at home

Lighting — the sun moved (the 21.06's baseline is part of the range, Level 2: the lighting
baseline is the lux at the demo's time, and the sun does not hold the baseline). The fix is
the baseline (the 21.06's "no direct sun in the camera cone" — the range's orientation, not
the detector's confidence), or the time (the demo is at the time the baseline was taken, or
the baseline is re-taken at the demo's time — step 1's power check is the re-take's
moment).

### Symptom: the sound shot fires at the wrong moment (the crowd claps early)

The cue is not a spoken word (the 21.07's runbook row: the cue is a spoken word — "clap
code now" — and the crowd claps *after* the word, not before). The fix is the runbook (the
spoken cue is step 10's first line, and the e-stop is in the operator's hand while the
crowd is clapping — the 21.09's operator row, the fatigue is the early clap). The
min-gap merge (21.07 Level 3) is the *detector's* guard against the echo; the spoken cue
is the *demo's* guard against the crowd.

### Symptom: the cannon arc clears the net (the 45° shot goes over)

The net is for the 6.1 m apex (Level 2), and the net is 4 m high (the net is the *indoor*
constraint, not the model's). The fix is the angle (the 29° of Level 2's computation —
apex 3.1 m, range 20.8 m — the boards are at 3-5 m, and the 20.8 m range is the *overshoot*,
which is the net's job: the net catches the 20.8 m, the boards catch the 3-5 m). The
outdoors day is the 45° day (the 25 m clearing, no ceiling).

## Common mistakes

- Demoting the matrix to the demo (the 10 demo shots are the *show*, not the *proof* — the
  72-shot smoke is the proof, and a combination that has not been smoked is not in the
  demo, Level 1).
- Letting the audience set the cue (the crowd claps early — the spoken word is the guard,
  and the e-stop is in the operator's hand while the crowd is clapping).
- Skipping the baselines because "it worked at home" (the lighting, the noise, the wind —
  the baselines are part of the range, Level 2, and the sun moves).
- Packing the evidence after the demo (the CSV is written *during* the demo — step 7's
  confirm + log is the row, and a demo without the CSV is a performance, not a test).
- Leaving the pack on the range (step 12's pack out — the 20.02 rule: the Li-ion is a fuel
  tank, and it does not sit on the range overnight with the audience's kids).

1. The Scout's smoke matrix is 72 shots. The demo is 10. Why is the 72-shot matrix the
   proof and the 10-shot demo the performance, and what would change if you demoed a
   combination that had only 2/3 in the matrix?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   The matrix is the *admission ticket* (Level 1's TIP): the 3-shot smoke catches the
   wiring errors (the 21.09-E3's rows) at 3 shots each, and the 10-shot demo is the
   *show* — the combinations that passed are performed in front of the audience. A 2/3
   combination is *allowed* (the pass is 2/3 or 3/3 — the 1/3 is the finding), but the
   demo's ≥ 8/10 hit rate is the *demo set's* criterion, and a 2/3 combination in the demo

   the 21.04 drag factor, the 21.03 flap angle) or it is out of the demo set (the demo is

   </details>

2. The pack V delta for the 30-minute show is 0.4 V (within the < 0.5 V pass). The firing
   was 10 shots (0.3% of the pack). Where did the 0.4 V go?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   The drive (Level 4: the 17 W average × 0.5 h = 8.5 Wh — the 37.8 Wh pack's 22%, and

   The firing's 0.3% is 0.11 Wh — a rounding error against the 8.5 Wh. The 0.4 V delta is
