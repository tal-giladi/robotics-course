## What you will learn

- Trace the launch chain for any launcher: **stored energy → efficiency → projectile energy → muzzle velocity → range → recoil**, with the numbers at every step.
- Size a **rubber-band slingshot** from the spring constant and stretch, and explain why the BOM's "4 J" is not the number your bands actually deliver.
- Compute the **linear-actuator cannon**: work = F·d, the 50% efficiency of a real actuator, and the recoil kick on the robot.
- Understand why the **servo throw arm is a lobber, not a rifle** — torque stalls the load, but range is tip speed = slew rate × radius.
- Handle the **CO2 magazine** as the one launcher whose energy is chemical, not electrical — and as the only pressurized part in the build.

## Why it matters

The static releasers of 21.03 let go; the launch mechanisms of this lesson *give*. Giving
energy to a payload is the moment the war machine becomes a war machine, and it is also the
moment the arithmetic stops being optional: a slingshot that is 0.60 J indoors is a
slingshot that is 0.60 J against a finger, and a cannon that is 6.0 J at 3 m is a cannon
that needs 25 m of clearing. The BOM's energy column ("~4 J", "~12 J", "~4 J") is what the
*store* says; the model in `code/projectile_range.py` is what the *physics* says, and the
difference between the two columns is the content of this lesson.

## Concept

Every launch mechanism is the same chain with different storage:

```text
    store energy ──efficiency──▶ projectile energy ──m = const──▶ v = √(2E/m)
                                                        │
                                    v² sin(2θ)/g ◀──────┘  (ideal, no drag)
                                          │
                                     × drag factor      (the air's tax)
                                          │
                                     RANGE ──▶ recoil: v·m_projectile / m_robot
```

The four launchers of the BOM are four different *stores*: rubber (elastic), a motor
(electrical work), a servo (rotational kinetic energy), a gas cartridge (chemical). The
chain is identical, and every number in it is either a datasheet value or a measurement
you make at the range.

> [!TIP]
> **Ask your teacher:** "The AGF servo has 78 kg·cm of torque — that is 7.6 N·m. The
> slingshot bands pull with maybe 15 N at the pouch. Why can't the servo just throw the
> ball like the bands do?" (Answer in Level 4: torque × angular travel = work. The servo's
> 7.6 N·m is real, but the arm only rotates a small angle before release, and the tip speed
> is set by the slew rate, not the torque. The bands win because the stretch is long.)

## Technical explanation

### Level 1 — where the energy goes (efficiency)

| Launcher | Store | Efficiency to projectile | Where the rest goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Slingshot | elastic, ½kx² | ~70% | band internal friction, snap losses, pouch drag |
| Cannon | electrical work, F·d | ~50% | motor losses, channel friction, ball compliance |
| Throw arm | rotational, ½Iω² | ~100% of what the motor puts in | the "loss" is the slew rate, not the transfer |
| CO2 magazine | chemical (12 g cartridge) | ~20-40% of the cartridge's energy | gas dynamics, the magazine's own blowback |

Two consequences. First, the BOM's stored-energy column overstates the projectile energy by
a factor of 1.4-5: the slingshot's "2-8 J" is bands, not ball; the cannon's "~12 J" is
work, not flight. Second, the CO2 magazine is the odd one out: its energy budget is not on
the weapon bus at all (the servo just pulls the trigger, ~2 A for 0.2 s), so the power
budget of 21.08 does not see it — the cartridge is the fuel.

### Level 2 — the slingshot (run the model)

```bash
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py slingshot
```

```text
slingshot  steel ball 5 mm (0.004 kg)
  stored 0.86 J  ->  0.60 J to projectile (70% eff)
  muzzle   17.4 m/s   ideal range    30.8 m   dragged   9.2 m
slingshot  paintball (0.00025 kg)
  stored 0.86 J  ->  0.60 J to projectile (70% eff)
  muzzle   69.6 m/s   ideal range   493.2 m   dragged 148.0 m
  band snap-back hits the frame with the stored 0.9 J in ~50 ms -> ~35 N impulse average: BOLT the frame, don't glue it
```

The model: two heavy 13 mm "tractor" bands in parallel give a total stiffness of
$k \approx 120$ N/m (estimate — measure yours with a spring scale: pull 100 mm, read the
force, $k = F/x$). At 120 mm stretch the stored energy is $E = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2 =
\tfrac{1}{2} \times 120 \times 0.12^2 = 0.864$ J; at 70% efficiency the projectile gets
0.60 J. A 5 mm steel ball (0.004 kg) leaves at $v = \sqrt{2E/m} = \sqrt{2 \times 0.60/
0.004} = 17.4$ m/s; the ideal (no-drag) range is $v^2/g = 30.8$ m, and the air's tax (drag
factor 0.30 for a 5 mm ball at this speed) brings it to ~9.2 m.

Two things to notice. First, the BOM's "2-8 J" for the slingshot corresponds to
$k = 110-550$ N/m — your bands are probably the 120 N/m end, not the 550. *Measure, don't
assume*: one spring scale and ten minutes separates the store's number from your number.
Second, the paintball row is a warning: the same 0.60 J into a 0.25 g paintball is 69.6 m/s
— the light payload is the fast one, and "the slingshot is kid-safe" is true only for the
steel ball, not for anything you can load into it.

### Level 3 — the cannon (run the model)

```bash
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py cannon
```

```text
cannon (120 N x 100 mm actuator)
  work 12.0 J  ->  6.0 J to projectile (50% eff)
  bouncy ball 4 cm (0.05 kg): muzzle 15.5 m/s   ideal 24.5 m   dragged 11.0 m
  recoil kick on scout (4.16 kg): 0.186 m/s
  recoil kick on assault (5.51 kg): 0.141 m/s
  recoil kick on siege (6.46 kg): 0.120 m/s
```

The 120 N actuator over 100 mm does $W = F \cdot d = 120 \times 0.10 = 12.0$ J of work;
at 50% efficiency (motor + channel friction + the ball's compliance — a bouncy ball is not
a rigid projectile) the ball gets 6.0 J and leaves at $v = \sqrt{2 \times 6.0/0.05} =
15.5$ m/s. Ideal range 24.5 m, dragged ~11 m (drag factor 0.45 — the ball is light and fat).
The BOM's "0.05 kg bouncy ball leaves at ~7 m/s" estimate was conservative; the model says
15.5 m/s, and 21.10's range clearing is set by the model with a 2× margin (25 m).

The recoil, by momentum conservation: $v_{kick} = v \cdot m_{proj}/m_{robot} = 15.5 \times
0.05/5.51 = 0.141$ m/s on the Assault. That is a 0.28 m/s² effective deceleration over
0.5 s — under the 1.0 m/s² drive limit, so the cannon's recoil is a stability nuisance
(the rail flexes, the LiDAR sees a 1 cm wobble), not a tipping event. But it is *real*:
gusset the rail at the actuator mount, and never fire the cannon while the robot is braking
hard in the same direction (21.01 Troubleshooting).

### Level 4 — the throw arm (run the model)

```bash
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py arm
```

```text
throw arms (servo tip speed is the limit, not the torque)
AGF 78 kg*cm, 0.15 kg magnet, r 0.30 m, 0.15 s/60deg
  I 0.0165 kg*m^2  energy 0.40 J  tip 2.09 m/s  ideal range 0.45 m
MG996R-360, 0.05 kg magnet, r 0.25 m, 0.12 s/60deg
  I 0.0051 kg*m^2  energy 0.20 J  tip 2.18 m/s  ideal range 0.49 m
  verdict: the heavy throw is a ~2 m/s LOBBER. Torque stalls the load,
  but range is set by tip speed = slew rate x radius. The BOM's '~4 J'
  entry is the energy the arm CAN put in at full speed; at real slew
  rates with a 0.15 kg tip it is ~0.4 J (this is the model, 21.04 Level 4).
```

The arm is a rotating body: $I = m r^2 + I_{arm} = 0.15 \times 0.30^2 + 0.003 = 0.0165$
kg·m² (payload + the arm's own inertia). The AGF's slew is 0.15 s per 60°, i.e.
$\omega = (60° \text{ in rad})/0.15 = 1.047/0.15 = 6.98$ rad/s, and the energy at release
is $E = \tfrac{1}{2} I \omega^2 = 0.5 \times 0.0165 \times 6.98^2 = 0.40$ J; the tip speed
is $v = \omega r = 6.98 \times 0.30 = 2.09$ m/s, and the range is 0.45 m.

Read that verdict slowly: **the AGF 78 kg·cm servo — the ₪1,247 part, the "siege" weapon —
throws a 0.15 kg magnet 0.45 m.** The torque (7.6 N·m) is real and it is what stalls the
load and swings the arm against gravity; but the range is set by tip speed, and tip speed
is slew rate × radius. The BOM's "~4 J" is the energy the arm *could* put in if it swung
fast — it does not swing fast; 0.15 s/60° is a 1/5-scale RC servo at full voltage, not a
turbine. The heavy-throw is a *lobber*: it puts a 0.15 kg magnet on a target 0.5 m away,
and the magnet (21.02 stick class) does the rest. If you want range, the slingshot is the
range weapon; if you want mass-on-target, the arm is the mass weapon.

```bash

```

```text
coga  BB 0.25 g at 100 m/s (estimate; chronograph yours)
  projectile energy 1.25 J   ideal range 1019 m   dragged ~51 m
coga  BB 0.20 g at 120 m/s (estimate)

  a 12 g paintball cartridge holds ~8-15 shots in a magazine of this class
  (estimate; the CYMA magazine is ~25 rounds — count yours).
```

The CYMA CO2 magazine is the highest-energy OTC weapon in the BOM (21.02): a servo pulls
its trigger (~2 A for 0.2 s — the only electrical cost), and the 12 g cartridge does the
rest. A 0.25 g BB at 100 m/s (estimate — *chronograph yours*: film two markers 1 m apart
with the phone at 240 fps and divide) carries 1.25 J. The ideal range is 1019 m — a vacuum
is a liar for BBs, which are feathers at 100 m/s — and the drag factor of 0.05 compresses
it to ~51 m. That is why 21.10 gives the CO2 day its own 50 m clearing and a windsock: the
BB's range is the most weather-dependent number in the module.

And the cartridge is the only part of the whole build under real gas pressure (~60-75 bar
for a 12 g paintball cartridge). 21.09 Level 3 covers the rolling-cartridge failure; the
one rule to remember from here: the cartridge is fuel, and fuel gets caged.

## Code

The launch model ships with the module. Run all six commands:

```bash
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py slingshot   # elastic store
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py cannon      # work = F·d
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py arm         # ½Iω², the lobber verdict
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py coga        # the CO2 magazine
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py recoil      # momentum budget
py 21-war-machine/code/projectile_range.py check       # all rules at once
```

The core of the model, as shipped in `code/projectile_range.py`:

```python
def elastic_energy_j(k_n_m: float, stretch_m: float) -> float:
    """Energy stored in stretched rubber: E = 1/2 k x^2. ``k`` is the TOTAL stiffness of the
    band set in parallel (2 heavy bands: ~120 N/m at this thickness, estimate — measure yours)."""
    return 0.5 * k_n_m * stretch_m ** 2


def muzzle_velocity_m_s(energy_j: float, mass_kg: float, efficiency: float = 1.0) -> float:
    """v = sqrt(2 E_eff / m): all of the (efficiency-weighted) energy becomes kinetic energy."""
    return math.sqrt(2.0 * energy_j * efficiency / mass_kg)


def ideal_range_m(muzzle_velocity_m_s: float, angle_deg: float = 45.0) -> float:
    """No-drag projectile range: R = v^2 sin(2 theta) / g. 45 degrees maximises it."""
    a = math.radians(angle_deg)
    return muzzle_velocity_m_s ** 2 * math.sin(2.0 * a) / G


def range_m(muzzle_velocity_m_s: float, drag_factor: float, angle_deg: float = 45.0) -> float:
    """Dragged range: ideal range x drag_factor. Factor is an empirical (estimate) per
    projectile size/density — a 6 mm BB is a feather compared with a 4 cm bouncy ball."""
    return ideal_range_m(muzzle_velocity_m_s, angle_deg) * drag_factor


def recoil_m_s(muzzle_velocity_m_s: float, projectile_kg: float, robot_kg: float) -> float:
    """Conservation of momentum: the robot gets v * m_projectile / m_robot of kick."""
    return muzzle_velocity_m_s * projectile_kg / robot_kg
```

## Troubleshooting

### Symptom: the ball doesn't leave the channel (cannon)

Most likely first: friction — the channel is 0.5 mm too tight for the ball (print shrink);
the actuator is fighting friction, not the ball (you can feel it: the current trace shows
the full 120 N never arriving at the ball). Then: the ball is seated proud of the channel
floor and the actuator's push plate is not concentric (it pushes at an angle). Then: the
actuator is stalling mid-stroke (sag — 21.08; or the 5 A fuse is the branch's limit and
the actuator wants 10-15 A — the BOM gives the cannon its own branch for exactly this).

### Symptom: the actuator stalls mid-stroke

Measure at the actuator plug, not the bus: if the voltage sags below 10 V during the stroke,
it is the pack + branch (21.08's sag model — run `sag --current 15`). If the voltage holds
and the current spikes, the channel is rubbing (Level 3's friction case). The stall current
is also why the cannon branch is 18 AWG and 5 A: a stall is a short that the fuse must
survive long enough to be diagnosed.

### Symptom: the bands snap (slingshot)

Fatigue: UV + cycles. Keep the bands out of direct sun (an Israeli summer car is 60 °C —
the bands are in the rail's shadow, not the boot), and rotate two sets (the BOM's "pack" is
a pack for a reason). A band that snaps at < 50 cycles is a bad band — the 13 mm "tractor"
spec varies between packs; the spring-scale k measurement is the acceptance test for a new
pack.

### Symptom: the cannon shoots left

The bore is not parallel to the rail: the ball rubs one wall and deflects. The fix is
mechanical (reprint the channel, or the actuator's push plate is bent), not aiming — a
cannon that aims by being bent is a cannon that stops aiming when it heats.

### Symptom: the CO2 cartridge rolls off the stand

The classic (21.09 Level 3): the 12 g cartridge is a 60-75 bar missile in a horizontal
position. Cage it (a printed holder with a 5 mm lip), never a spare on the deck, vent spent
ones outside. If it rolls in the demo, the demo is over — the room is now a de Laval
nozzle's target.

## Common mistakes

- Quoting the BOM's energy column as projectile energy (the "~4 J" slingshot is the bands'
  stored energy; the ball gets 0.60 J).
- Designing the throw arm for range (it is a mass-on-target weapon; the range is 0.45 m and
  that is *fine* — the magnet sticks).
- Trusting the CO2 magazine's "100+ fps" class without a chronograph (fps is marketing,
  m/s at your altitude and temperature is the number).
- Letting the cannon fire while the robot brakes (recoil + braking = a 0.4 m/s² event that
  the 1.0 m/s² limit allows, but the rail flex is what breaks, not the limit).
- Forgetting that the slingshot's snap-back hits the *frame* (35 N for 50 ms): the frame is
  bolted to the rail, and the rail is bolted to the deck — a glued frame is a frame that
  leaves.

## Knowledge check

1. The slingshot's bands store 0.864 J. The projectile gets 0.60 J. Where do the missing
   0.26 J go, and how would you measure it?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   Into the bands (internal friction — they heat, by a fraction of a degree), the pouch
   (drag on the ball for the first 50 mm of the launch), and the snap-back (the bands'
   kinetic energy after the ball leaves is real energy that hits the frame). Measure it:
   the frame's impulse (a load cell under the Y, or the phone's accelerometer on the
   rail) integrated over the snap = the snap-back energy; the rest is the difference.
   </details>

2. Why is the throw arm's range (0.45 m) not a function of the AGF's 78 kg·cm torque?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   Because range is set by tip speed at release, and tip speed = slew rate × radius. The
   torque decides whether the arm can swing against the payload's weight (it can, by a
   wide margin — 7.6 N·m against a 0.15 kg × 0.30 m × 9.81 m/s² = 0.44 N·m load) and how
   fast it *accelerates* the arm, but the release happens at the slew-rate-limited speed,
   not the torque-limited one. The torque is the headroom; the slew rate is the ceiling.
   </details>

3. The CO2 BB's ideal range is 1019 m and its dragged range is ~51 m. Why is the drag
   factor (0.05) so much smaller than the bouncy ball's (0.45), when the BB is faster?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   Because drag force scales with area × velocity² and the deceleration is F/m: the BB is
   6 mm (area ~2.8×10⁻⁵ m²) and 0.25 g, so the drag deceleration at 100 m/s is enormous
   relative to its momentum (it is a feather at that speed); the bouncy ball is 4 cm
   (area ~1.3×10⁻³ m², 45× the BB's) but 0.05 kg (200× the mass), so its momentum
   outlasts the drag. The factor is not "fast = more drag" — it is "area-to-mass ratio",
   and the BB's is 70× worse.
   </details>

4. Your cannon fires the bouncy ball at 15.5 m/s. The demo is indoors, under a 3 m ceiling.
   Can you fire at 45°? What is the apex, and what angle do you use instead?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   No. Apex at 45° = v²·sin²(45°)/(2g) = 240×0.5/19.62 = 6.1 m — the ball clears the
   ceiling by 3 m. For a 3 m ceiling: sin²θ ≤ 2×3×9.81/240 = 0.245, so θ ≤ 29°. At 29°
   the range is v²·sin(58°)/g = 240×0.848/9.81 = 20.8 m (still more than the room —
   indoors, the cannon is a ceiling problem first and a range problem second; the
   21.10 net exists for the outdoors day).
   </details>

5. The recoil on the Assault is 0.141 m/s. Why is that "a nuisance, not a tipping event"
   (21.01), and when does it stop being true?
   <details><summary>Answer</summary>
   Because the tipping limit of the Assault is 8.05 m/s² of deceleration (21.01's `mass`
   output) and the recoil bleeds off over ~0.5 s, i.e. 0.28 m/s² — a third of a third of
   the limit. It stops being true when the cannon fires *and* the robot brakes hard in the
   same instant (0.28 + 1.0 = 1.28 m/s², still under 8.05, but the rail flex is now a
   dynamic load the 21.01 static model does not see), or when the robot is on the
   boundary (the arm extended forward — 21.08 Level 1's extended-arm case, where the
   braking limit is 1.35 m/s² and the recoil eats a fifth of it).
   </details>

## Practical challenge

**The range day.** Set up a 20 m range (a court, a parking lot, the schoolyard — with
permission). Fire: 10 slingshot shots (steel balls, 3 m and 10 m targets), 10 cannon shots
(bouncy ball, 45° and 30°), 10 CO2 shots (BBs, 10 m board) — if the Scout does not have the
cannon/CO2, do the slingshot ×10 twice and record the package. Log every shot in the CSV
of 21.10 (t, launcher, payload, angle, pack V, range/impact, hit/miss). Deliverable: the
refit drag factors for the three projectiles (your measured ranges, not the table's), the
apex measurements (phone slow-mo on the cannon's 45° shots), and the one-sentence verdict
per launcher: rifle, lobber, or ceiling-problem.
